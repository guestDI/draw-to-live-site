Privacy Policy

Effective October 27, 2026

Draw a Game is a drawing toy for children. It has no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. We do not ask a child for a name, an email address or any personal information.

Who we are

Draw a Game is made by Dzmitry Ihnatovich. For anything about privacy, email ignatovich.dm@gmail.com.

What stays on the iPad

Monsters, the drawings of their parts and of their things, names, a drawing put aside for later, the places they have visited, settings chosen in the grown-up corner, and voice recordings a child makes for a monster are stored only on the iPad. Voice recordings never leave the iPad. Photos a child takes of a monster are saved in the app and, with permission, added to the Photos app; the app can only add photos and cannot read your library.

Magic pictures: the one thing that is sent

The optional "Magic pictures" button paints a snapshot of the monster scene as a storybook picture. It is off until a grown-up turns it on in the grown-up corner; once it is on, a child can press it after taking a photo. The snapshot is a screenshot of what is on the iPad screen, not a camera photo of a person. When the button is used, that snapshot is sent over an encrypted connection to our server (hosted on Cloudflare), which passes it to an AI image service (OpenRouter and the image model it uses) and returns the painted picture. We do not keep the snapshot or the painted picture on our server. The image services process the image under their own terms. Our server counts how many pictures have been painted so the free taste and the monthly allowance can be kept, and Cloudflare may keep ordinary technical logs of requests, as any web host does.

Subscriptions

A grown-up can subscribe to Magic pictures. Purchases are handled by Apple. We use RevenueCat to confirm that a subscription is active and to restore it. RevenueCat receives an anonymous identifier and purchase information, and starts only when a grown-up opens the subscribe screen, never during a child's play. The same anonymous identifier is sent with a picture so the monthly allowance can be counted. We never receive your name, Apple ID or payment details.

What we and RevenueCat keep

On the iPad's Keychain: how many free pictures have been used, a random identifier for the iPad, and, after a purchase or restore, the anonymous subscriber identifier. On our server: counts of pictures painted, against that anonymous identifier for subscribers, and for the free taste against the iPad's random identifier and, for one day, the network address the request came from, to keep the free pictures fair, plus a shared daily count. At RevenueCat: the anonymous identifier and purchase information. None of it contains a name, an email address or anything a child typed.

Grown-up gate

The subscribe screen and settings are behind a grown-up check, so children cannot reach them by accident.

Children

The app is made for children aged 3 to 8 and is designed not to collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has given us personal information, email us and we will delete it. A parent can also ask us what we hold or ask us to delete it, by email.

Deleting data

Delete the app to remove the monsters, drawings, photos and recordings stored on the iPad. We hold no other copy of them. The small Keychain record described above stays on the iPad after the app is deleted, so that the free pictures do not start over. To have the counts we hold, and the record at RevenueCat, deleted, email us. Our records use only an anonymous identifier, so we may ask for your purchase receipt to find it.

Changes and contact
